Mathematical Modeling Lab

Ideas across disciplines

A collaborative environment for mathematical discovery, biological insight, and student development.

Lab mission

Model the mechanism. Understand the system.

The Mathematical Modeling Lab studies how interactions among individuals, populations, environments, and technologies generate collective dynamics. We build models that are mathematically tractable, biologically or behaviorally grounded, and informed by data whenever possible.

The lab welcomes collaborations that connect theory with experiment and that give students meaningful ownership of research—from formulating questions and building models to analysis, computation, and publication.

Core principle

Collaboration as a method

Important questions rarely fit within one discipline. Our projects connect applied mathematics with biology, ecology, agriculture, public health, psychology, engineering, and data science.

How we work

A research cycle grounded in mechanism

01 · Frame

Identify the organizing question

Translate biological or behavioral hypotheses into explicit states, scales, feedbacks, and measurable outcomes.

02 · Build

Develop the right model

Choose continuous, discrete, stochastic, spatial, network, agent-based, or hybrid formulations to match the mechanism.

03 · Analyze

Reveal thresholds and regimes

Use stability, bifurcation, sensitivity, simulation, and inference to explain persistence, collapse, transitions, and control.

04 · Connect

Integrate data and experiment

Parameterize and test models with collaborators, then use discrepancies to sharpen both theory and experimental design.

05 · Translate

Generate actionable insight

Relate mathematical structure to pollinator management, conservation, public health, or team design.

06 · Mentor

Develop independent scholars

Support students in becoming confident modelers, communicators, collaborators, and research leaders.
