Research program

Complex adaptive dynamics

Rigorous mathematics, data, and interdisciplinary collaboration for biological, ecological, and human systems.

Research vision

Mechanism, emergence, and prediction

Professor Kang develops mathematical frameworks for systems in which individual behavior, environmental variation, and multiscale feedback generate unexpected collective outcomes. Her work combines nonlinear analysis with computational modeling, statistical inference, machine learning, and experimental or observational data.

Across projects, the research asks three linked questions: Which mechanisms organize system behavior? Where are the thresholds between resilience and collapse? How can models guide intervention, management, or design?

Mathematical toolkit
  • Ordinary, partial, delay, and stochastic differential equations
  • Discrete-time and integro-difference systems
  • Network, agent-based, and multiscale models
  • Bifurcation, stability, persistence, and optimal control
  • Machine learning and AI-integrated dynamical systems
Program 01

Pollinator health and colony resilience

Mathematical models connect colony-level processes to landscape and agroecosystem drivers.

Honey bees

Multiple stressors across scales

Current work examines how heat, pesticides and fungicides, Varroa mites, viruses, nutrition, seasonality, and foraging behavior interact to shape colony growth, thermoregulation, and survival.

Plant-pollinator systems

Climate variation and sustainability

New theory addresses threshold criteria and dynamical regimes under multiscale temperature variation, linking pollinator decline to the resilience of ecological and agricultural systems.

Program 02

Social insects as complex adaptive systems

Colonies provide powerful model systems for understanding decentralized organization.

Division of labor

How task allocation, interaction networks, and metabolic demands scale with colony size and changing environments.

Collective transitions

How recruitment, quorum thresholds, heterogeneity, and information flow shape migration, defense, and foraging.

Eco-evolutionary feedback

How cooperation, competition, social organization, and life-history tradeoffs influence colony performance and evolution.

Program 03

Population, ecological & evolutionary dynamics

Research on predator-prey and food-web systems, positive interactions, strong Allee effects, spatial ecology, seasonality, stochasticity, adaptation, and persistence.

  • Critical transitions, bistability, and resilience
  • Spatial dispersal and heterogeneous environments
  • Eco-evolutionary and epidemiological feedback
  • Resource management and conservation
Program 04

Health & social systems

Mechanistic and AI-enabled models reveal how individual decisions, peer influence, social structure, and policy interact in epidemics and behavioral health.

  • Infectious disease and eco-epidemiology
  • Adolescent substance-use dynamics
  • Obesity and social behavior
  • Natural-resource and human interactions
Human-autonomy interaction

Trust, reliance, and communication in adaptive teams

This program combines dynamical systems and machine learning to identify how trust evolves, how reliance decisions emerge, and how communication and feedback can improve team effectiveness in automated environments.
