Mentoring

Developing scholars

Research mentoring that combines intellectual independence, collaborative practice, and sustained professional support.

Mentoring philosophy

Ownership grows through practice

Professor Kang’s mentoring approach gives students increasing ownership of the full research process: identifying an important question, translating mechanisms into mathematics, selecting appropriate tools, interpreting results, and communicating the work to different audiences.

Mentoring is individualized and interdisciplinary. Students enter with different technical preparation and career goals; the shared expectation is curiosity, rigor, reliability, and a willingness to learn across disciplinary boundaries.

Mentoring framework

From first question to independent contribution

Discover

Start from a meaningful problem

Students learn to distinguish a broad topic from a researchable question and to identify the mechanism that mathematics can clarify.

Build

Strengthen technical foundations

Reading, analysis, computation, and writing are developed in direct service of the research rather than as isolated exercises.

Collaborate

Work across expertise

Students engage with mathematicians, biologists, data scientists, and domain experts while learning to communicate assumptions precisely.

Lead

Own the intellectual work

As confidence grows, students lead model development, analysis, presentations, manuscripts, and new project directions.

Communicate

Share results clearly

Conference talks, posters, papers, and interdisciplinary discussions help students explain both technical results and broader significance.

Advance

Prepare for the next stage

Mentoring includes fellowship strategy, professional networks, recommendation letters, career planning, and continued collaboration.

Student outcomes

A community of continuing impact

Former mentees have advanced into faculty positions, postdoctoral appointments, government research, industry, and data-science careers. Many continue to collaborate and mentor others, extending the community beyond a single degree or institution.
